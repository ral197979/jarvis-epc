# Competitive Positioning

> An honest assessment of where Denver Engineering wins, where it ties, and where it doesn't compete — against the major platforms in adjacent categories.
>
> This document is deliberately candid. Overstating capability loses enterprise deals at the technical-evaluation stage; knowing exactly where you win lets sales steer to winnable ground.

---

## The category problem

Denver Engineering doesn't fit cleanly into one existing software category, which is both its biggest advantage and its biggest sales challenge. Buyers come looking for one of four things:

1. A **CMMS/EAM** (maintain operating assets) — Maximo, UpKeep, Limble, eMaint, Brightly
2. A **facilities/workplace platform** (manage occupied buildings) — FM:Systems, FacilityForce, ServiceNow Facilities
3. A **construction project-controls / document platform** (build the facility) — Procore, Aconex/Oracle, Autodesk
4. A **commissioning platform** (verify the facility) — niche Cx-specific tools

Denver Engineering's real category is **#3 + #4 fused, plus an intelligence layer** — *deliver the facility and prove it's ready to operate.* Its winning move is to own the seam between "build it" and "operate it" that none of the four categories covers well.

## Strengths (where we are genuinely differentiated)

1. **Build-to-operate seam ownership.** The only platform purpose-built for *delivery + commissioning + readiness + turnover* as one connected flow.
2. **Commissioning depth on shared data.** 4-phase workflow, 21 asset types, test packs, deficiency/retest loops, and readiness scoring — wired into finance, documents, and the executive view.
3. **Readiness as a first-class metric.** A transparent, weighted, continuously computed score with blocking factors and history. Few competitors have anything equivalent.
4. **Grounded AI, not a bolt-on chatbot.** Cited answers from your own corpus with a prompt-injection guard, engineered to refuse rather than hallucinate.
5. **Explainable decision support.** Rules-based, transparent recommendations — important for engineering and government buyers who distrust black boxes.
6. **Predictive + portfolio intelligence.** Health scoring, EAC forecasting, anomaly detection, root-cause correlation, and cross-project conflict detection in one place.
7. **Auditability and security by design.** Row-level security on every record and automatic audit logging — strong for government and regulated buyers.

---

## Gaps (where we are honestly weaker)

1. **Not an EAM/CMMS.** No parts inventory or labor-costed work orders. *Mitigation: integrate with Maximo/SAP; the Action Center covers task management.*
2. **No real-time SCADA / plant operations.** Not a process-control, alarm-management, or live-HMI system. *Mitigation: position for delivery + commissioning; integrate with OT.*
3. **Design calculators are drafting aids, not certified engines.** Water/wastewater/HVAC/stormwater sizing is design-assist only. *Mitigation: lead with P&ID/PFD generation (which is genuinely strong) and project/Cx capabilities; never present these numbers as stamped engineering.*
4. **No space/occupancy/workplace management.** Out of scope vs. facilities platforms.
5. **Smaller ecosystem and mobile maturity than Procore.** Fewer prebuilt integrations and a less mature native mobile app today. *Mitigation: open integration framework + webhooks; offline field capture exists.*
6. **No subcontractor network effect.** No marketplace/network like Procore's. *Mitigation: compete on depth and intelligence, not network.*

## The honest one-paragraph positioning

> Denver Engineering is not trying to be your CMMS, your SCADA system, or your facilities platform. It is the system of record and intelligence layer for **delivering complex capital projects and proving they're ready to operate** — the commissioning-and-turnover seam where projects lose the most money and where horizontal tools are weakest. It wins by fusing project controls, commissioning depth, financial controls, document AI, and predictive portfolio intelligence on one auditable platform, and it integrates cleanly with the EAM, OT, and facilities systems that own the phases before and after. Buyers who want a lightweight maintenance app, a plant-operations system, or certified discipline calculators should look elsewhere; buyers who want to deliver and commission mission-critical facilities with confidence and evidence should look here.
