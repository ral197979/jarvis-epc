# ROI Analysis

> The business case, with worked examples across six facility types. For each: current state, future state with Denver Engineering, and the savings, risk reduction, and efficiency gains.

---

## How to read these numbers

The figures below are **illustrative models** built from widely cited industry benchmarks (e.g., capital-project overrun rates of 20–80%, rework costing 5–12% of contract value, and weeks of professional time spent on manual reporting and document search). They are **directional planning tools for a customer business case — not guarantees and not derived from a specific deployment.** Each scenario states its assumptions so a customer can substitute their own numbers.

The platform's commercial model is a tiered subscription (Starter / Professional / Enterprise) with an annual discount and enterprise add-ons (dedicated success management, certification evidence packages, deployment support). Even at enterprise pricing, the software cost is a small fraction of the value at stake on a single mission-critical project — which is the core ROI argument.

**The universal ROI thesis:** on a capital project, the cost of the platform is dwarfed by the cost of *one* avoided overrun, *one* avoided disputed turnover, or *one* avoided rework event. The platform pays for itself by preventing a single bad outcome.

## Scenario 1 — Embassy / Government Secure Facility

**Context.** A $120M secure diplomatic facility with stringent compliance, security, and documentation requirements.

**Current state**
- Commissioning and compliance evidence assembled by hand for security accreditation
- Disconnected tools; manual, security-sensitive document handling
- Audit preparation consumes weeks of staff time
- Handover disputes over what was tested and verified

**Future state with Denver Engineering**
- Air-gapped deployment option for classified environments
- Automatic audit logging and exportable compliance evidence (SOC 2 / ISO 27001 alignment)
- Structured commissioning with full evidence and readiness scoring
- Role-based access and row-level security demonstrating data governance

**Illustrative value**
| Lever | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Compliance/audit effort reduction | ~70% less manual evidence assembly |
| Commissioning acceleration | 2–4 weeks earlier accreditation readiness |
| Dispute/rework avoidance (1.5% of $120M) | ~$1.8M risk avoided |

**Risk reduction.** Security accreditation backed by defensible, auditable evidence; reduced risk of accepting an unready facility.

## Scenario 5 — Water / Wastewater Treatment Plant

**Context.** A $90M municipal water/wastewater plant delivered for a public utility owner.

**Current state**
- Owner can't objectively verify the plant is ready to accept
- Commissioning of process equipment (clarifiers, RO skids, dosing skids, UV, blowers, lift stations) tracked on paper
- Disputes over performance testing and turnover completeness
- Operations inherits incomplete asset and test records

**Future state with Denver Engineering**
- Commissioning across the exact process asset types the platform supports (clarifier, RO skid, dosing skid, UV system, chlorination system, lift station, blower, pump, valve, instrument, PLC)
- Objective readiness score for owner acceptance with blocking factors
- Full turnover package (O&M, spares, training, warranty) handed to plant operations
- IoT sensor monitoring with threshold alerts during commissioning

**Illustrative value**
| Lever | Estimate |
|---|---|
| Commissioning acceleration | 2–4 weeks earlier acceptance |
| Rework/retest reduction (1.5% of $90M) | ~$1.35M |
| Operations onboarding | Day-one asset register + searchable O&M via Ask Jarvis |

**Risk reduction.** Evidence-based owner acceptance; clean risk transfer at handover; reliable operating record for the utility.

> **Honest scope.** The platform delivers and proves the readiness of the plant and hands over the record. It is **not** the SCADA/DCS that operates the plant day to day — it integrates with that operational layer. Its discipline design calculators are drafting aids, not certified process-sizing engines.

## Building your own business case

1. **Start with the value at risk**, not the software cost. One avoided overrun or disputed turnover typically exceeds years of subscription.
2. **Pick your three biggest levers** (usually commissioning acceleration, overrun avoidance, and productivity).
3. **Apply your own benchmarks** to the levers table above.
4. **Add the soft value** — auditability, knowledge retention, and executive confidence — which often tips the decision even when hard ROI already clears the bar.

**The conservative case:** on any single mission-critical project, avoiding **one** bad outcome — a missed energization date, a disputed acceptance, a compounding overrun — returns multiples of the platform's annual cost.
