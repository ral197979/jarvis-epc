# Executive Summary

> **Denver Engineering** — The AI-Powered Command Center for Capital Project Delivery, Commissioning & Operational Readiness
>
> *Built by Ava Systems LLC · Enterprise SaaS · Multi-tenant · Production-hardened*

---

## What the platform is

**Denver Engineering is the single operating system for delivering complex capital projects — from the first proposal to the final turnover package — and for proving they are ready to operate.**

It unifies the work that today is scattered across a dozen disconnected tools: project controls, document management, design coordination, field reporting, commissioning, financial controls, and executive reporting. On top of that unified data, it layers an AI assistant that answers questions from *your* documents (not the open internet) and a predictive intelligence engine that tells leaders which projects are slipping **before** the slip shows up in a monthly report.

In one sentence: **Denver Engineering replaces the binder, the spreadsheet, and the email thread with a live, auditable, AI-assisted system of record for the entire project lifecycle.**

---

## Who it serves

Denver Engineering is built for organizations that **deliver, commission, and hand over** mission-critical facilities:

- **EPC contractors** building data centers, water/wastewater plants, industrial facilities, and institutional buildings
- **Owners and operators** (data center operators, utilities, hospitals, universities, government agencies) who need to know a facility is genuinely ready before they accept it
- **Commissioning authorities (CxA)** who must prove every system was tested, every deficiency resolved, and every asset turned over with evidence
- **Operations and facility leaders** who inherit the facility and need the as-built record, the test history, and the asset register on day one
- **Executive leadership** who need a single, trustworthy view of portfolio health, cost, schedule, and risk

## Why customers buy it

1. **It collapses the toolchain.** One platform instead of project controls + document management + a commissioning tool + BI dashboards + a knowledge base.
2. **It makes "ready" objective.** The readiness score turns a subjective argument into a number with a list of exactly what's blocking turnover.
3. **It sees problems early.** Predictive health and anomaly detection surface slippage while there's still time to act.
4. **It remembers everything.** A grounded AI assistant turns thousands of documents into instant, cited answers.
5. **It's auditable by design.** Every mutating action is logged automatically — the evidence package builds itself.
6. **It's enterprise-ready.** Multi-tenant, role-gated, row-level security on every record, JWT auth, and continuous audit.

---

## Key differentiators

- **Lifecycle depth + commissioning depth in one product.** Most project-controls tools stop at construction. Most commissioning tools don't do project controls. Denver Engineering does both, on shared data, so a failed functional test instantly shows up in the project's readiness and the executive's portfolio view.
- **Grounded AI, not a chatbot bolted on.** Ask Jarvis answers only from your ingested documents and shows the source for every claim — designed to refuse, not hallucinate.
- **Readiness as a first-class metric.** A transparent, weighted score (open actions, blockers, SLA health, inspections, escalations) computed continuously, with history and blocking-factor breakdowns.
- **Decision support that explains itself.** The recommendation engine is rules-based and explainable — every "escalate / reassign / prioritize" suggestion comes with the reason and the impact, not a black-box score.
- **Built-in auditability and security.** Row-level security on every tenant record and automatic audit logging on every change — compliance is a byproduct of normal use.

### Five-minute executive briefing
> **The market.** Capital project delivery is a multi-trillion-dollar global activity that runs on tools that don't talk to each other. Industry studies consistently put schedule and budget overruns on large projects in the 20–80% range, and a major driver is the same root cause: fragmented information and a commissioning/handover process run on paper. The cost of *getting it wrong* — rework, disputed turnover, a facility that isn't actually ready — dwarfs the cost of the software.
>
> **The platform.** Denver Engineering is a multi-tenant SaaS platform that unifies the full project lifecycle on one data model. Operations teams manage projects, daily logs, actions, and timesheets. Engineering teams manage drawings, RFIs, submittals, BIM coordination, and design deliverables. Construction teams run inspections, punch lists, and field work. Finance teams run budgets, change orders, cost control, earned value, and portfolio rollups. Commissioning authorities run the full four-phase Cx workflow with test packs, deficiencies, and turnover packages.
>
> **The intelligence layer.** Three things make it more than a database. First, **Ask Jarvis** — a grounded AI assistant that turns your entire document corpus into instant, cited answers and refuses to make things up. Second, **Predict** — health scoring, earned-value-based cost forecasting, and anomaly detection across the portfolio. Third, the **Executive & Operations Command Center** — a live portfolio risk heatmap, SLA compliance, escalation hotspots, contractor performance, root-cause event correlation, and explainable next-best-action recommendations.
>
> **Why it wins.** It is the only category of product that treats *readiness to operate* as a first-class, measurable outcome — bridging the gap between the people who build the facility and the people who run it. Commissioning depth plus project-controls depth plus grounded AI, on one auditable platform.
>
> **Why it's safe.** Enterprise multi-tenancy with row-level security on every record, JWT authentication, automatic audit logging on every change, rate limiting, and a prompt-injection guard on the AI. Compliance evidence is a byproduct of normal use, not a fire drill.
>
> **The ask.** Start with one program or one facility. Stand up the project, ingest the documents, run the commissioning workflow, and put the executive dashboard in front of leadership. Within one project cycle you'll have a single source of truth, a provable commissioning record, and an early-warning system for the rest of the portfolio.
